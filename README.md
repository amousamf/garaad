# Garaad

Maths and English games for children aged 3 to 12. *Garaad* is Somali for "wisdom". (The app was called Tiri before.)

The mascot is Garaad, a wise owl whose eyes are the double "aa" of the name. Brand files and decisions are in the Kids Learning App project (`claude/garaad-brand.md`).

**Play:** https://garaad.app (the old address, https://amousamf.github.io/Tiri/, moves each phone's saved games here once, then forwards). About and Privacy pages: `about.html`, `privacy.html`.

- The first time it opens on a phone, three short welcome screens introduce the owl, what *garaad* means and the app.
- Add each child by name, age and owl (16 owls: four colours, each with a cap, glasses, a star or a bow); the games for their age group appear automatically.
- Each child picks a colour theme: Savanna sun (default), Ocean blue or Night sky. Grown-up screens use one calm style.
- Stars can be spent on hints: 2 stars remove some wrong answers, or show a first step for typed answers. Answers found after a hint are not counted as right first time.
- 10 games per age group (3–5, 6–8, 9–12), each with 10 levels that unlock in order.
- Questions are read aloud for younger children.
- **English:** a Maths / English switch on each child's games page, with 21 games in five steps: 1 *Listening ears*, 2 *Sound play*, 3 *Words and letters* (Opposites, Word Sort, Letter Hunt, Trace the Trail), 4 *Reading* (Caption Catch, Yes or No Island, Story Steps, Read and Answer) and 5 *Writing* (Missing Letter, Spell the Picture, Spelling Bee, Sentence Builder, Fix-It Doctor). Younger children see Steps 1–3 first; ages 6–12 see Reading and Writing first. Every word learned grows a flower in the child's Word Garden.
- Speaking games use the microphone. Recordings stay on the phone and are never uploaded; a grown-up can always confirm an answer.
- Installs to the phone's home screen and works offline after the first visit.
- Progress stays on the device. Parents can send results by email or WhatsApp from the Parent area.
- **Parent gate:** the Parent area, removing a child, sending results and the About/Privacy links ask the grown-up to type a number written in words (for example "seven, two, five, one" → 7251), in English, Af-Soomaali or Arabic. Young children can't read it. After a pass it stays open for 2 minutes; removing a child always asks.
- **Rewards:** when a level earns new stars, a "+3 ★" badge pops up and flies to the child's star count. Finishing today's practice opens a gift box once a day with 2 or 3 bonus stars.
- **Tools in every game:** hear the question again, get a hint, and **Words on/off** (hide the written question for children who listen; it is still read aloud; only offered when the game speaks).
- **Check button:** in thinking puzzles (Odd One Out, Patterns, Fix-It Doctor, Read and Answer) the child taps an answer, can change it, then presses **Check**. Quick drills keep instant feedback.
- **Games page rows:** *Start here* (today's practice) at the top, then rows that scroll sideways: Numbers, Adding and taking away, Shapes, sizes and measures, Thinking puzzles, Adventures (the play-style games) and Challenges. English keeps its five steps as rows. **Brain teasers** (puzzles from the next age group) appear once a child has done today's practice or gets 85% right over 30+ questions (families only).
- **Tiny stories:** every game has a hero who travels along the progress bar to a goal (the squirrel to its nuts, the rocket to the moon). The owl says the story at the start, and when the level is passed the hero arrives, or drives off.
- **Hand help:** if a child aged 3–8 (or any child at school) doesn't tap for 7 seconds, the question is read again and a hand shows how to answer: it glides over the choices, or from the answer box to the keypad and the tick. It never points at the right answer, and shows at most twice per question.
- **Learning profile:** after adding a child, three quick questions for the grown-up: what to work on (Maths, English or both), how the child feels about learning, and 5, 10 or 15 minutes a day. The answers set the first subject, how many games are in today's practice (2, 3 or 4), and a slightly easier quick check for a child who finds learning hard. A profile card in the Parent area shows this, with the child's strongest game, and can be changed any time.
